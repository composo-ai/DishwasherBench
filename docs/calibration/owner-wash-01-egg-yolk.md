Run on the evening of 2026-10-10. This is the plan as given to the owner; the results go into a wash
record (`docs/calibration/wash-record.md`) when they arrive.

Programme: first planned for Auto 45-65; changed to Eco 50 the same evening, when it was decided to
score the game on Eco (fixed course, printed 4:55, 9.5 L, 0.836 kWh, no sensor decisions). Auto
stays a secondary, uncalibrated programme, so the question below about Auto is left
for a later wash; on Eco the model predicts well-placed oven-dried egg plates come out clean, and this
wash tests that with the owner's capsule.

## Why this wash first

On the Auto programme the simulation leaves every item soiled with oven-dried egg yolk dirty however it
is loaded, while test institutes rate Auto as high as Eco on sister machines. Desk research could not
decide which of reaction rate, enzyme survival, the oven-drying penalty, the amount per plate or the
wash length is wrong (`docs/research/auto-recommendation.md`). Oven-dried against air-dried plates in
one wash separate the ageing penalty from the rest; a turned plate of each kind tests whether facing
matters for a chemistry-limited soil, on which the research disagrees with itself.

## The plan

- Soil: raw egg yolk only (no white), whisked. About half a level teaspoon (about 3 g wet) per plate,
  spread thin and even over the flat centre of the plate with the back of the spoon, rim left clean.
- Plates: four identical plates (two at the least).
- Drying: two plates oven-dried for 2 hours at 80 C and cooled (the standard laboratory ageing); two
  plates air-dried on the counter for the same 2 hours. If only one kind, oven-dried.
- Loading: lower basket, rear rows, stood as the owner normally stands plates (eating face to the
  middle), an empty slot either side of each. One plate of each kind turned round to face the side
  wall. Nothing large in front of them. The rest of the load as usual, or empty.
- Wash: one Finish Powerball Ultimate capsule in the dispenser, Eco 50, no options, alongside the
  owner's normal evening load.

| Plate | Drying | Facing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | oven, 2 h at 80 C | to the middle (normal) |
| 2 | oven, 2 h at 80 C | turned to the side wall |
| 3 | air, 2 h | to the middle (normal) |
| 4 | air, 2 h | turned to the side wall |

## What to record

- Before: a photograph of each plate face-on; a photograph of the loaded basket; which slots.
- The time the display shows at the start, and the clock times of start and finish (which branch of
  the sensor-controlled programme ran).
- After, before wiping: a photograph of each plate face-on in good light, flash off; per plate whether
  it is clean, has a faint film, or has specks or patches, and where. Dried yolk feels matt and rough
  under a fingertip.

## What each outcome would mean (on Eco 50)

- Oven-dried plates clean: agrees with the anchored model on Eco with the owner's capsule; the kinetics
  and the end of a deposit are about right at 50 C.
- Oven-dried dirty, air-dried clean: the ageing penalty and the kinetics are roughly right; levels
  should use air-dried soils where a clean result must be reachable.
- Turned plates dirtier than their partners: facing matters for chemistry-limited soils in this
  machine, and the supply of fresh liquor to a sheltered face is weaker than the model has it.
- Everything dirty: the model is too optimistic on Eco with this capsule (enzyme level, ageing penalty,
  or less spraying in the long soak than modelled: the manual hints at intermittent spraying).
